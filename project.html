The Project

Philosophical and methodological foundations of research into the origins of the universe, life, and mind.

About

Cosmology, the biological search for the origin of life, and the archaeological quest for the origin of the human mind share important methodological features and challenges.

These include a pronounced under-determination of theories based on indirect evidence of deep history, ubiquitous selection effects and biases, an inherent interdisciplinary orientation, and a crucial assimilation of techniques from other disciplines.

Given their striking commonalities, philosophers and scientists are increasingly analyzing common structures of historical explanations across sciences studying the deep past and the origins of the physical universe, life, and mind.

How do we construct reliable knowledge about events and processes that cannot be directly observed?

Interdisciplinary exchange

Sciences of the Origin is a place for exchanging ideas between cosmologists, archaeologists, and biologists working on origins, as well as philosophers, historians, theologians, and social scientists studying the various aspects of human thought that have shaped these intellectual pursuits.

Reflecting on how methodologies and ideas from different domains of knowledge are intertwined in these sciences can yield valuable methodological and philosophical insights.

Four methodological challenges

Indirect evidence

Research into the deep past often depends on traces and present-day evidence rather than direct observation of the historical events themselves.

Selection effects

What survives into the present and what becomes available to researchers is shaped by multiple forms of selection and bias.

Interdisciplinarity

Questions about origins frequently cross disciplinary boundaries and require the integration of different kinds of evidence and expertise.

Methodological transfer

Techniques developed in one field can become important tools for investigating origins in another.
